## Executive directive

Use `Migraine Tracker` as the customer-facing product brand. Keep the accepted App Store title `Migraine Tracker- Headache Log` and subtitle `One Tap Diary & Trigger Alerts` for the next metadata experiment. Replace only the US hidden keyword field with the exact 96-byte field below. The title contains the only high-demand core phrase in the tracked category, `migraine tracker`, and it improved from approximately #37 to #29 after the current title shipped. A punctuation-only title change is not worth mixing into the next keyword test.

Replace the current five iPhone screenshots with six canonical frames after the keyword experiment has a stable measurement window. The existing set has strong visual polish, but it violates the fleet screenshot contract, uses inconsistent fixtures, spends a slot on a dark-mode duplicate, and makes several causal or predictive claims that the literal UI does not prove. The first three replacement frames must tell a complete story: preserve details, capture the moment in one tap, then prove the surrounding context was saved.

Keep the separate Apple Watch listing and add a second native Watch state that proves a successful log. Do not create an iPad set because the app is iPhone-only.

Prioritize Germany, Japan, Russia, Thailand, and China for the next regional metadata work. These are the strongest recent, comparable non-US storefronts after removing a malformed catch-all analytics export. Do not use the raw fleet funnel summary to select Turkey, Sweden, Uzbekistan, or Kyrgyzstan until the duplicated April-to-August batch is repaired.

This is a health and wellness app. Acquisition copy may describe logging, attached context, descriptive patterns, alerts based on the user’s history, and shareable exports. It must not imply diagnosis, treatment, prevention, guaranteed forecasting, causal trigger identification, or proof of what helps.

## Product and feature truth

The defensible acquisition promise is:

> Log a headache with one tap, let optional Health and weather context attach to the event, review descriptive patterns across your history, and export a clear record.

Verified product capabilities include:

- One-tap headache logging on iPhone
- Apple Watch logging and confirmation
- Widget and Siri entry points
- Optional Apple Health context
- Optional location-based weather and environmental context
- Barometric pressure, temperature, air quality, and related environmental values when available
- Optional severity and notes
- Event history
- Descriptive patterns from accumulated logs
- CSV export and a doctor-report export path
- Proactive alert configuration based on the user’s recorded history and forecast signals
- Local-first data storage without a required account
- Light and dark appearance

Do not claim that the app:

- Diagnoses migraine or another condition
- Determines the medical cause of a headache
- Proves that a specific factor triggered an attack
- Treats, cures, prevents, or relieves headache or migraine
- Predicts a migraine with certainty
- Identifies what “actually helps”
- Replaces a clinician, medical record, or emergency care
- Provides structured medication tracking unless that feature is added and verified

The description, screenshots, onboarding, paywall, website, support copy, and app UI should use the same informational disclaimer. Suggested short form:

> Patterns and alerts are informational and do not diagnose, treat, prevent, or guarantee a migraine.

This field deliberately omits words already present in the title and subtitle: `migraine`, `headache`, `tracker`, `log`, `one`, `tap`, `diary`, `trigger`, and `alerts`. Apple can combine visible and hidden terms, so repeating them would waste the limited field.

The change removes:

- `aura`: relevant to some migraine users, but tracked `migraine aura` is approximately #106 and autocomplete evidence is weaker than diary and journal intent.

The change adds:

- `journal`: Apple autocomplete confirms `migraine journal` and `headache journal` intent. It creates new compounds without duplicating `diary`.
- `export`: a literal feature and a high-trust conversion message. The tracked `headache export` rank is approximately #29, even though autocomplete is weak.
- `pattern`: the core retention and paid-value mechanism. It creates `migraine pattern`, `headache pattern`, `diary pattern`, and `trigger pattern` combinations.

Do not add `relief`, `treatment`, `cure`, `diagnosis`, or `medication`. They either create false product intent or increase health-claim risk.

## Competitive positioning

The generic migraine category has large incumbents. Migraine Buddy dominates broad diary and trigger intent. Pressure Pal owns explicit barometric forecast positioning. Other newer trackers compete on manual diary, medication, trigger, and doctor-report language.

Migraine Tracker should not claim to out-diagnose or out-predict those apps. Its defensible distinction is lower logging friction plus automatic context:

1. Tap once at the moment a headache occurs.
2. Optional Health and environmental context attaches without a long form.
3. The history becomes more useful over time.
4. Patterns stay descriptive and tied to sample counts.
5. Exports make the record portable.

Use `One tap, then context fills in` as the internal positioning test. It is more specific and defensible than `find your triggers` or `get ahead of the next one`.
